Fabric captures how work really gets done by talking to the people who do it. It turns those conversations into a living picture of each process, so knowledge no longer lives only in people's heads.

## How knowledge gets in

On any process, a contributor can share what they know in one of three ways:

- Start an AI interview, where a voice interviewer asks questions and follow-ups.
- Record a voice note in the browser.
- Upload existing audio files, such as a meeting recording or voice memo.

Fabric transcribes the audio, analyzes it, and keeps the result as a contribution on that process.

## What Fabric builds from it

Each process has five tabs:

- **Overview**: a short, evidence-backed brief of how the process works.
- **Contributions**: the conversations the brief is built from.
- **Process Flow**: a diagram of the steps, decisions and handoffs.
- **Insights**: where to investigate or improve, such as bottlenecks and handoffs.
- **Automations**: ideas for what could be automated, with a brief you can copy.

Departments and functions get their own overviews that roll up the processes beneath them. You can also download a PDF report for a process.

## The Function, Department, Process hierarchy

Your organization's work is arranged in three levels, shown in the Process Tree:

- **Functions** are the broad areas of the business, such as Finance or Operations.
- **Departments** are the teams inside a function.
- **Processes** are the work itself, such as approving invoices.

Every finding in Fabric stays connected to the conversations it came from, so you can trace a claim back to its source.

## What Fabric is not

Fabric describes what contributors report. Its findings are not approved policy, and they are not a substitute for formal compliance evidence.

## Getting oriented

Open the Help menu (the question-mark icon in the header) to watch the 2-minute tour or choose Show me around for a guided walkthrough of the workspace.
